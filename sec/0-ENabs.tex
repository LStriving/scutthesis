\begin{abstractEN}
Deep learning has achieved remarkable progress in general video action understanding and analysis in recent years.
However, migrating these technologies to the healthcare sector reveals a pronounced domain gap.
Unlike natural video analysis, medical video analysis frequently suffers from low image contrast, subtle motion amplitudes, and highly imbalanced data distributions. 
These challenges impair the ability of deep models to learn effective visual representations for accurate action analysis.
Therefore, deeply integrating medical domain priors to achieve more efficient medical video action understanding significantly 
facilitates the practical deployment of smart healthcare systems.

Focusing on two typical types of medical video data, 
videofluoroscopic swallowing study (VFSS) videos for auxiliary diagnosis and glaucoma surgery videos 
for intraoperative navigation, 
this dissertation thoroughly investigates the core challenges of medical video action understanding in complex clinical scenarios:
(1) In temporal micro-action localization for VFSS videos, 
the low contrast of X-ray images and the blurring and occlusion of key anatomical structures,
which easily overwhelm weak motion signals with static background noise,
making it difficult for models to capture precise temporal boundaries.
(2) In online action detection for glaucoma surgery videos, 
the significant imbalance in surgical phase durations and the high visual similarity 
among different operations frequently cause missed detections and false judgments during real-time inference.

To address these challenges, this dissertation presents the following research:

For VFSS video analysis, this dissertation proposes a novel framework, \fullnameen (\sexyname), 
to tackle blurred anatomical structures and obscured weak target features in temporal micro-action localization.
By introducing a skeleton heatmap modality based on clinical priors, 
the framework provides the model with robust geometric structural references, 
guiding it to learn highly interference-resistant visual representations.
Furthermore, the proposed method employs a \crossblockfullen 
and a \channelblockfullen to efficiently calibrate and enhance multimodal features.
These modules use skeleton features to guide spatial focus and explicitly strip redundant static background signals, 
effectively amplifying subtle motion signals.
Experimental results on the VFSS benchmark dataset show that 
the proposed method improves mean Average Precision (mAP) by 5.3\% over state-of-the-art methods 
and exhibits excellent generalization on an external validation set.

For glaucoma intraoperative navigation, this dissertation proposes \fulltwoen (\sexytwo), 
an online action detection framework that addresses temporal logic confusion 
and imbalanced data distribution in online surgical action detection.
By leveraging the structural priors of surgical procedures through a \graphmodulefullen, 
the method enhances the model's perception of surgical state transitions and action durations,
guiding it to learn logically coherent scene representations.
Additionally, the framework introduces a \lossfullen to balance feature learning for long- and short-duration actions.
Extensive experiments demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms state-of-the-art approaches, 
achieving online detection accuracy improvements of 0.9\% on 
the self-constructed minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS) dataset and 1.9\% on the public OphNet dataset.

\end{abstractEN}
