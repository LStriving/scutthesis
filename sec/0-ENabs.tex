\begin{abstractEN}
In recent years, deep learning has achieved remarkable progress in general video action understanding and analysis tasks.
However, a pronounced domain gap persists when migrating these technologies to the healthcare sector.
In clinical practice, the analysis of medical video compared to natural video is frequently hindered by low contrast, 
subtle motion amplitudes, and highly imbalanced data distributions, 
making it difficult for deep models to learn effective visual representations for precise action analysis. 
Therefore, exploring how to deeply integrate medical domain priors to achieve more efficient action understanding 
for medical videos is of great significance for the practical deployment of smart healthcare systems. 
Focusing on two typical types of medical video datam, 
videofluoroscopic swallowing study (VFSS) for auxiliary diagnosis and 
glaucoma surgery videos for intraoperative navigation,
this paper deeply explores the common bottlenecks and specific challenges 
of medical action understanding in complex clinical scenarios:
(1) In temporal micro-action localization for VFSS, 
the low contrast of X-ray images and the blurring or occlusion of key structures 
cause weak action signals to be easily submerged by static background noise,
making it difficult for models to capture precise temporal boundaries; 
(2) In online action detection for microsurgical videos, 
the significant imbalance in the duration of surgical phases and the high visual similarity 
between different operations lead to missed detections and misjudgments during real-time inference. 
To address these challenges, the research contents of this dissertation are as follows:

For Video Fluoroscopic Swallowing Study, 
this dissertation proposes a novel framework named \sexyname
to address the problems of blurred anatomical structures 
and submerged weak target features in temporal micro-action localization,
which introduces a skeleton heatmap modality based on clinical priors to provide a robust geometric reference for the model, 
thereby guiding the learning of more interference-resistant visual representations. 
Furthermore, to ensure efficient calibration and enhancement of multimodal features, 
the method utilizes a Cross-modal Feature Calibration module (\crossblockshort) 
and a Channel-level Enhancement mechanism (\channelblockshort). 
These modules guide spatial focus via skeleton features and explicitly strip redundant static 
background signals to achieve effective amplification of subtle action signals. 
Experiments on the VFSS benchmark dataset demonstrate that the proposed method 
achieves a 5.3\% improvement in mean Average Precision (mAP) over existing methods, 
and its superior generalization capability is further validated on an external validation set.

For glaucoma intraoperative navigation, 
this dissertation proposes an online action detection framework named \sexytwo 
to address the issues of temporal logic confusion and uneven data distribution 
in online surgical action recognition. 
This method aims to utilize structural priors of surgical procedures 
through a Dynamic Logic Evolution module (\graphmoduleshort) 
to improve the model's perception of surgical state transitions and action durations, 
thereby guiding the learning of more logically coherent scene representations. 
Additionally, a Class Boundary Weighted Loss (\lossshort) is introduced to 
promote balanced feature learning for long and short-term actions. 
Extensive experiments on the newly constructed MIGS dataset and the public OphNet dataset 
show that the proposed method achieves 0.9\% and 1.9\% improvements 
in online recognition accuracy compared to the baseline methods, respectively.
\end{abstractEN}
